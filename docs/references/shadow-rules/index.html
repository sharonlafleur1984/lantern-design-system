Shadow rules

One rule for every shadow in Lantern, so the Popover's drop shadow never happens again. Today there are two shadow systems side by side: the glass cast that follows the page's light (D69), and three static drop shadows per theme (rest, hover, float) that always fall straight down. This page makes them one.

Your turn

  • Look at the two mocks under rule 3: the three heights lit from one light, beside the static shadows of today.
  • Decide whether the three heights are the right three, and whether rule 5's mapping of each component to a height reads right.
  • Then say yes, and this becomes decision D73 and a section of the Light and glass page, with the token change and the Popover as the first component moved over.

What React Aria gives us, and what it does not

React Aria ships no shadows: it is unstyled on purpose, so its parts carry behavior and we carry the look. What it does give us is the list of things that float above the page, each with a name and a role: Popover, Menu, Tooltip, Dialog and Modal, plus the Select's menu built on Popover. That list is the backbone of rule 5. The physics in rules 1 to 4 come from D69 and from how a real light casts a shadow, not from a library.

The rules

  1. One light casts every shadow.

    A shadow is the glass cast from the page's light (D69): it falls away from the light, in the theme's cast color, at the theme's strength. No component has a shadow of its own. The three static shadow tokens, rest, hover and float, retire.

    Today nine components still carry a static shadow. The Popover's is the one you saw: a 16px drop straight down while everything around it leans with the Orb.

  2. Three heights, and nothing in between.

    Every surface sits at one of three heights above the page, and its shadow says which. Resting: in the page's flow, a short cast and a tight contact shadow. Raised: lifted toward the light, twice the distance and wider; a hover or a press does this. Floating: above the page, four times the distance, widest and faintest; anything React Aria floats.

    Three is enough to tell apart at a glance and few enough to keep consistent. Material uses more levels; we do not have more kinds of surface.

  3. Higher is farther, softer and fainter.

    Each height scales the same cast: the distance doubles at each step, the blur grows with it, and the tint thins, because a shadow cast from higher up spreads out. The direction never changes within a page. The numbers below are the draft scale, with D69's resting values as the base.

    HeightDistanceBlurCast tint, light modeContact
    Resting1 × the theme's cast distance (4px)1 × (40px)1 × the theme's castYes
    Raised2 ×1.5 ×1.2 ×Yes, fainter
    Floating4 ×2 ×1.4 ×No

    The mocks are CSS, not the renderer. The direction and the growth are the point; the exact blur and tint come from each theme's glass tokens and would be captured in Storybook before anything merges.

  4. Night is stronger, day is softer, and the theme sets the tint.

    The cast is the theme's own: Spotlight's deep red, Sunrise's warm ink, Fireside's teal, at the theme's strength. Dark mode keeps its stronger cast and light mode the soft one you picked on Oct 20. The three heights scale those values; they never add a color of their own.

  5. Every component has one height, and the floating ones are React Aria's.

    HeightComponentsToday
    RestingCard, Tile, Action tile, Callout, Select's field, Checkbox box, Switch track, Tag, Toggle button group, Settings sheet at restMost carry the glass cast since #163; Callout, Settings and Switch still carry a static rest shadow
    RaisedAny of the above while hovered or pressed; the Orb's ballButton, Tile and Action tile already lift toward the light on hover; Select's field lifts since #163
    FloatingPopover, Select's menu, Menu, Tooltip, Dialog, Modal, the Settings sheet when open; anything React Aria places in a portalPopover and Select's menu use the static float shadow

    A component never picks a shadow. It says which height it is at, and the height picks the shadow from the light.

  6. Settings change the strength, never the direction.

    Increase contrast makes the cast stronger and adds the 3 to 1 line under the rim (D69). Reduce transparency changes nothing about shadows. Reduce motion keeps the light still, so shadows stay still. Forced colors draw borders instead.

  7. One place in the code, checked by a test.

    The three heights are three classes on the shared glass edge, ds-glass-edge--raised and ds-glass-edge--floating beside the resting default, each setting the cast distance, blur and tint multipliers. A test fails when a component stylesheet uses box-shadow with anything but the glass cast, so a new component cannot bring its own shadow.

What it would take

  • Tokens: three multipliers in the base (distance, blur and tint per height), no new value per theme. The three static shadow tokens are deleted once the last component is moved, with the lock updated.
  • Code: two classes in glass.css; the Popover, Select's menu, Callout, Settings and Switch move over, one pull request for the floating ones and one for the resting ones. Dark mode keeps its own cast values as it does today.
  • Checks: the stylesheet test above, and the screenshots of every moved component in every theme, light and dark, for your tick.
  • Docs: this page as a section of Light and glass, decision D73 updating D69, and each moved component's Storybook page naming its height.
